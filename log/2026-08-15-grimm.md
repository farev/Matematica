# 2026-08-15 — grimm

**Target.** Grimm's conjecture (1969): certified verification far past the
2006 Laishram–Shorey record (n ≤ 1.9236701629×10¹⁰), plus the first census
of *critical intervals* — gap members all of whose prime factors are at most
the gap length, with explicit Hall matchings and exact deficiency margins.
Goal: all n ≤ 10¹², a 52× extension, on 4 cores in an afternoon.

**Result.** **CERTIFIED** — Grimm's conjecture holds for all n ≤ 10¹²
(52× the 2006 Laishram–Shorey record; 52.1 min of 4-core wall time), with
the first census of critical gap members: 18,575,022 members in 18,400,995
gaps, each factored, matched to a prime ≤ k, with exact Hall margins; no
margin is negative; **all 133 Hall-tight gaps below 10¹² are prime-power
tight** — interaction tightness, the only mechanism by which Grimm could
actually fail, never occurs in range; the largest tight gap holds
31⁸ = 852,891,037,441 (k = 109), confirming a pre-data prediction. Plus
**PROVED** (elementary): the reduction lemma (folklore, no novelty
claimed) and Proposition 5.2 — margin-≤-0 gaps occur infinitely often
(2^a, a ≡ 3 mod 6). Controls: π(10⁸..10¹²) five-for-five exact; six
first-occurrence maximal gaps reproduced; exhaustive sympy re-derivation
on three windows and 250–300 sampled gaps per chunk, plus a light pass
over all 18.6M rows — zero errors anywhere.

**What failed.**
- The first engine draft had a real bug — gaps spanning segment boundaries
  would have consulted a recycled smooth-part array — caught in code
  review before any run; the streaming-candidate fix is simpler anyway.
- `mine_stats.py` crashed on a name-shadowing bug (`dec` as function and
  dict) on first production use; fixed, rerun (mines already-verified
  data, so no exposure).
- One hallucinated factorization (with a stray CJK character) appeared in
  a NOTE draft for the max-L member; caught in self-review, recomputed
  (614,487,453,811 = 7·11·139·263·419·521), and turned into a
  documented lesson in WRITEUP.
- The selection-time back-of-envelope for census size was off ~30×
  (10⁵ predicted, 18.6M actual): it ignored prime powers of tiny bases and
  ordinary-length gaps at low heights. Consequence: full censuses stay out
  of git (hashes committed, regeneration one 52-min command).
- No primary source could be read all day (egress proxy) — every citation
  is (secondary), flagged in every document, with the re-verification list
  in NOTE §7.

## The three-candidate external slate (step 2)

Vetted by three parallel research agents (WebSearch-only; all quotes
secondary). Full reports in the session transcript.

**E1 — Grimm's conjecture (number theory).** Statement: if n+1,…,n+k are all
composite, there exist distinct primes p₁,…,p_k with pᵢ | n+i (C. A. Grimm,
Amer. Math. Monthly 76 (1969) 1126–1128; Guy UPINT §B32). Checked
2026-08-15 via search snippets of Wikipedia/MathWorld/primepuzzles/WorldSci
(secondary). Why open/room: verification record is n ≤ 1.9236701629×10¹⁰
(Laishram–Shorey, IJNT 2 (2006), "approximately one week" of one 2006 core —
per a tertiary AI-encyclopedia snippet, so (secondary²) on the runtime);
still cited as the record everywhere found; **not** subsumed by prime-gap
tables to 4×10¹⁸ — no explicit "Grimm holds when k ≤ f(n)" theorem bridges
(even with constant 1, the Ramachandra–Shorey–Tijdeman (log n/log log n)³
bound gives ≈1404 < 1476 at the max gap below 4×10¹⁸). No 2020–2026 work on
this corner found; the adjacent Erdős #375 corner (distinct multiples,
van Doorn–Li–Tang arXiv:2603.28636) is being actively strip-mined, Grimm
itself is not. Not an erdosproblems.com entry under its own name (it is
Grimm's problem, not Erdős's); nearest entry #375.

**E2 — Undirected repetition threshold URT(22) (combinatorics on words).**
Statement: URT(k) = inf of r such that undirected r-powers (xyx′ with
x′ ∈ {x, xᴿ}) are avoidable over k letters; Currie–Mol (TCS 866 (2021),
arXiv:2006.07474) proved URT(k) ≥ (k−1)/(k−2) for k ≥ 4, conjectured
equality, confirmed k = 4..21; every k ≥ 22 open (checked: no later
extension found; Shur's 2024 entropy-compression bounds are non-constructive
d+O(1), not exact values). A session would search for the morphism f₂₂ and
certify — but the f₄..f₂₁ construction family lives in a paper unreadable
today; flying blind on the ansatz.

**E3 — Ruzsa numbers R_m past m = 100 / Erdős–Turán interval thresholds
(additive combinatorics).** R_m: least r such that some A ⊆ Z_m has
A+A = Z_m with every element having ≤ r ordered representations.
Sándor–Yang (Acta Arith. 180 (2017)) did m ≤ 35; Ding–Li–Li–Niu–Zhao
(arXiv:2606.11069, **June 2026**) "provide exact values of R_m for all
m ≤ 100"; upper-bound race active (R_m ≤ 128, arXiv:2607.06167, July 2026).
Open: m ≥ 101, and nobody ships certificates. Related interval version sits
under Erdős problem #28 ($500). Why not today: the incumbent group is
racing with unknown tooling (scoop-prone), and the interval-threshold
novelty check requires reading Borwein–Choi–Chu (Math. Comp. 75 (2006) —
their branch-and-bound may contain the small-m table internally), which is
egress-blocked today.

Also vetted and rejected: peaceable queens a(16) (open since 2018, 2014 ILP
gap 37–64, resisted MIP/CP/SAT for 8 years — not a day target); queens
domination γ(Q₂₆) ∈ {13,14} (proof side out of reach; Rostami–Bright
arXiv:2508.11945 own the certified-SAT toolchain there); mixed van der
Waerden w(2;3,20) (last solved case w(2;3,19)=349 reportedly cost ~196
CPU-years in 2011-era search — 3–5 orders out of budget); balanced-sequence
threshold RTB(13)=12/11 and abelian repetition threshold ART(11) > 9/8
(both genuinely open and PROVED-shaped, but implementation-heavy against
unreadable primary sources).

**Today's attempt, precisely.** Verify Grimm's conjecture for all n ≤ 10¹²
by exhibiting, for every maximal prime gap with left prime p < 10¹², an
explicit system of distinct prime representatives — reduced to a matching
on the gap's *critical members* (members whose prime factors all are ≤ the
gap length k), since members with a factor > k are automatically safe.
Achieved means: CERTIFIED "no counterexample below 10¹²" (52× the 2006
record) with a committed census of every critical member (factorization,
assigned prime, exact Hall margin per gap), reproducible by two independent
implementations, plus the census statistics themselves (where tight gaps
live, how criticality thins with height) as new structural data. A Hall
failure, should one appear, would be a counterexample to Grimm's conjecture
— reported loudly, checked to death, and celebrated.
