# 2026-08-14 — graham-105

**Target.** Graham's $1000 problem (erdosproblems.com #376; Erdős–Graham–
Ruzsa–Straus, Math. Comp. 29 (1975) 83–92): are there infinitely many n with
gcd(C(2n,n), 105) = 1?  By Kummer's theorem the qualifying n are exactly those
with base-3 digits ≤ 1, base-5 digits ≤ 2, base-7 digits ≤ 3 (OEIS A030979).
The question is open and provably beyond any finite computation; what a
session can move is the problem's entire computational evidence base: the
known-terms census (published frontier: complete to 10^70, Thompson 2015),
the companion conjecture that n = 3160 is the last term also coprime to 11
(Graham, OEIS comment 2007), and the count data behind the N^0.02595
heuristic that is the reason Graham picked the infinite side. Plan: a
top-down search on leading base-3 digits with interval pruning in bases 5
and 7, aiming to push the complete census from 10^70 to 10^250 or beyond,
with the count-vs-heuristic table and the 1155 companion verified along the
way.

## Selection

Chose **C1** on the mandate's criteria. (a) Bottleneck: pure enumeration —
once the right algorithm is used. The terms have density ~N^0.026, so a
top-down DFS on leading base-3 digits, pruning whenever the almost-fixed
top base-5/base-7 digit strings already violate the caps, visits ~125
nodes per term found rather than the ~N^0.63 leaves of any bottom-up scan.
The whole published history of the problem fits in seconds of this
sandbox's time, and the marginal cost of each further order of magnitude
is ~6% more nodes. (b) Already done? The ledger is the OEIS b-file
(complete to 10^70, Nov 2015) plus Pomerance's papers; the vetting agent
specifically hunted for anything beyond 10^70 and found nothing.
(c) Extends: Thompson's census and Alekseyev's before it, the count data
behind Pomerance's heuristic (AMM 2015 §4, Integers 2026), Graham's 1155
finiteness remark, with results landing in OEIS A030979 and
erdosproblems #376's data notes. C2 was the runner-up and remains a good
future target; ties were to be broken toward the new, but C1 won outright
on (a) and (b).

What counts as achieving it: a certified complete census of A030979 to at
least 10^200 (vs 10^70), the count table it implies, and the 1155
companion pushed to the same height. Graceful degradation: the census to
whatever height the ladder completes, each rung a standalone certificate.

**Mid-session correction, logged as such.** The session's first two hours
were spent building and validating a *bottom-up* engine (Gray-code walk
over sums of distinct powers of 3 with staged mod-5^8/mod-7^6 filters,
32768 resume-safe tasks to (3^45−1)/2 ≈ 1.48·10^21), on the belief —
supported by the OEIS *display* line, whose last visible term is
≈ 2.5·10^16 — that the known frontier was 2.5·10^16. The literature agent
then established the b-file's true extent (10^70), which made that entire
campaign a replication of the bottom 10^-49th of the known range. The
campaign was stopped at a clean prefix (all n ≤ 3.66·10^19 > 3^41,
subsuming Alekseyev's entire reported 2008 range) and repurposed as the
independent bottom-range verification of the top-down engine that replaced
it. The cost of the wrong frontier assumption was ~2 h of wall time and
~90 CPU-minutes; the artifact it left is genuinely useful (a second,
algorithmically unrelated engine agreeing exactly on 62 terms and every
count to 3.66·10^19).

## Result

**CERTIFIED.** Complete census of A030979 below 3^600 ≈ 1.87·10^286:
**585,823,270 terms**, extending the published complete-to-10^70 frontier
(1374 terms, Thompson 2015) by **216 orders of magnitude** and the term
count ×426,363. Every rung of the ladder (3^148, 3^200, 3^250, 3^300,
3^350, 3^400, 3^500, 3^600) is a standalone certificate with fingerprints;
the 3^600 rung is a composite of 3753 + 4906 exhaustive tasks whose node
accounting reconciles exactly (73,906,945,791 tree nodes; the merged
histogram's cumulative counts reproduce all seven independently-run lower
rungs to the digit). The largest known term of A030979 is now a 286-digit
number (`data/max600.txt`; the previous largest published term has 66
digits). Total ladder cost ≈ 12.5 core-hours.

**CERTIFIED.** n = 3160 remains the last n < 3^600 with C(2n,n) coprime
to 1155 — Graham's finiteness prediction, verified 216 orders of
magnitude beyond the published census (and ≥ 182 beyond even the
unconfirmed "10^104" search mentioned in one snippet). NUMERICAL
companion: under the independence heuristic the expected number of
further 1155-terms was ≈ 0.81, with 0.72 of it below n = 60,000 — the
observed "none" has probability ≈ 0.44, so the prediction stands on
structure, not luck, and computation beyond this point adds little.

**CERTIFIED.** The first counts of G beyond 10^70, for every k ≤ 600
(`data/counts600.txt`), revealing that **G grows in rare bursts**: only
82 of 601 base-3 lengths carry terms; **[3^474, 3^530) is entirely
term-free** — a certified 26.7-decimal-order desert — after which
39,030,864 terms appear at length 531 alone; 82.6% of all terms below
3^600 have base-3 length ≥ 564. Local 50-level band exponents swing
0.0144–0.0408 around Pomerance's heuristic 0.02595. NUMERICAL: the
global fit G(N) ≈ 48.2·N^0.0248 over 3^100..3^600 — the independence
heuristic's exponent survives on average while its local independence
model fails qualitatively. We could not source any prior observation of
the burst structure (counts at these heights did not exist), but
priority must be checked against Pomerance's papers once they are
readable.

**CERTIFIED (replication).** Thompson's 2015 frontier re-derived from
scratch (exactly 1374 terms ≤ 10^70, his b-file count, including a(1)=0),
and the census below 3.66·10^19 > 3^41 (Alekseyev's reported 2008 range)
independently reproduced by the session's bottom-up Gray-code engine —
zero discrepancies on terms, counts, or 1155 flags.

Verification: five implementations across two algorithm families,
node-count- and fingerprint-identical on every overlapping range
(C ≡ Python top-down at L = 35/148/200/250/300; 4-worker task mode ≡
full mode at L = 300 to the last node); every emitted term passes an
independent digit + Lucas validator (3670 sampled across the 3^600
campaign, plus all 10,215 committed terms below 3^200, plus every
max-mode extreme — 0 failures); external anchors (OEIS display terms,
Thompson's 1374, Graham's 3160) reproduced, never assumed.

Verification architecture: five implementations (brute force ≤ 2·10^7 with
literal gcd(C(2n,n),105) checks via math.comb; Python bottom-up DFS;

## What failed

- **The bottom-up campaign** (see mid-session correction): right code,
  wrong target. Its 5.1-hour full plan would have "extended" a frontier
  that was actually 49 orders of magnitude further out. The failure was
  in step 2 of the survey — treating an OEIS display line as the extent of
  knowledge before the b-file was checked. Vetting agents ran concurrently
  with engine-building, which limited the damage to the cost of one
  refactor; serially, checking first would have cost less.
- **A self-matching pkill** killed the session's own shell mid-migration —
  the exact hygiene failure the 08-13 log warned about, repeated. The
  resume-safe task design absorbed it (zero recomputation lost beyond the
  killed workers' in-flight tasks).
- **Python recursion at L = 300** was launched as a background anchor and
  ran for 25+ minutes against the C engine's 18 s; killed after the L=250
  anchor had already given the Python≡C equality at scale. Python's role
  is validation, not production, and the session briefly forgot that.
- The first C engine draft had three real bugs (a wrong-stride cast that
  misindexed base-7 threshold comparisons, task prefixes mis-decoded when
  the split depth was not a multiple of 4, and no node-reconciliation
  path), all caught by the planned validation gates before any production
  run — none reached a certificate.
- **The depth-130 task split made task 0 the entire [0, 3^470) subtree** —
  a multi-hour single-threaded tail. Fixed mid-run by re-splitting that
  subtree into its own 4906-task campaign (mathematically identical:
  the thresholds satisfy W(600, 130+d) = W(470, d)); `merge600.py`
  audits the composite. Cost: ~25 wasted core-minutes of the discarded
  partial task.
- **Two shell-orchestration stumbles**: an ambiguously-backgrounded
  compound command silently killed a worker and launched sub-workers
  before their tasks file existed; and several status checks ran without
  `cd` after the harness reset the working directory, reading phantom
  paths. Both were caught by re-inspecting with absolute paths; neither
  touched any certificate. Single-purpose commands with explicit paths
  from here on.
