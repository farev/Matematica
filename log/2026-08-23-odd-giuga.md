## The three-candidate slate

Three directions, surveyed in parallel by three scouts (WebSearch only).

### Candidate 1 — smallest open queens-domination case (graph domination)

γ(Q_n), OEIS A075458: exact values known for all n ≤ 25 (snippet-verified
OEIS data line, 25 terms, ending 13, 13); smallest open case is
**γ(Q_26) ∈ {13, 14}**. Independent domination i(Q_n) (A075324) is known
through n = 31 (Langlois-Rémillard–Müßig–Roldán, arXiv:2211.05651 v3,
Mar 2025, Gurobi ILP), smallest open **i(Q_32)**. The frontier is hot:
Rostami–Bright, "Queen Domination by SAT Solving" (arXiv:2508.11945,
Aug 2025) certified n ≤ 18 with DRAT proofs, found a bug in the previous
n = 16 enumeration (371 non-isomorphic minimum sets, not 43), and closed
enumeration at n = 19 — and still did not touch γ(Q_26).
*Why open:* a 2025 paper states γ(Q_n) known only for n ≤ 25; three
modern-tooling groups (UNIDOM 2017, ILP 2022–25, SAT+DRAT 2025) stopped
short of 26. *Scoring:* (a) compute-breakable — NO for γ(Q_26): the hard
direction is refuting 13 queens on 26×26; the ILP relaxation is provably
stuck at the ⌈n/2⌉ bound (Karandikar–Dutta 2024) and cube-and-conquer
specialists capped certification at n = 19, so this smells like
CPU-months; i(Q_32) is softer but (b) races two currently-active groups
with stronger solvers than this sandbox has. (c) would extend
Rostami–Bright / LMR. All (secondary).

### Candidate 2 — weak Schur numbers (additive Ramsey theory)

WS(5) ∈ [196, 220] (lower: Eliahou et al. 2012 SAT witness; upper:
Bornsztein's ⌊k!·k/e⌋), conjectured 196; WS(6) ≥ 646 (Ageron et al.,
arXiv:2112.03175, weak templates), WS(7) ≥ 2146, WS(8) ≥ 6976 (Rowley,
INTEGERS 2021, algebraic constructions). *Why open:* Heule (S(5)=160,
2 PB DRAT proof) explicitly names WS(5) as the next SAT challenge and
expects it to be harder than S(5). *Scoring:* (a) NO for WS(5)=196
(harder than a 14-CPU-year proof); heuristic record-beating on WS(6)/WS(7)
means out-searching template constructions that replaced search entirely —
implausible on 4 cores. The scout's redirect — generalized weak Schur
tables (Ahmed–Boza–Revuelta–Sanz 2023: WS_3(3)=94, WS_4(3)=259, next
cells open, SAT-decidable) — is feasible, but (b) an August 2026 arXiv
paper ("Restricted generalized Schur numbers", 2608.08789) is active in
exactly that family and unreadable from here, so the scoop check cannot be
completed; and it would be this repo's third session in the
Schur/Rado micro-industry. All (secondary).

### Candidate 3 — odd Giuga numbers (computational number theory)

A Giuga number is a composite n with p | (n/p − 1) for every prime p | n;
equivalently (proved in this session's NOTE) a squarefree composite with
Σ 1/p − Π 1/p ∈ ℕ. Thirteen are known, all even; whether an odd one
exists is open, and a counterexample to Giuga's 1950 primality conjecture
would have to be an odd Giuga number with extra Carmichael conditions.
Recorded bound: an odd Giuga number has **at least 14 prime factors**
(secondary: MathWorld/Wikipedia, attributing BBBG, "Giuga's conjecture on
primality", Amer. Math. Monthly 103 (1996); an earlier bound of 9 is also
quoted). *Why believed open/stale:* six differently-phrased searches found
no post-1996 improvement; the 2013 successor computation (Borwein–
Maitland–Skerritt, Integers 2013: conjecture counterexample ≥ 4771 prime
factors, > 10^19907) improved only the Carmichael-constrained record, its
companion code repository (github.com/matt-sk/Giuga-Exclusion-Bounds) has
been an empty "Coming Soon" placeholder since 2014, and 2026 activity
(Alekseyev Aug 2026: A054377 exhausted below 10^24; Wang Apr 2026: first
9-factor primary pseudoperfect number, arXiv:2605.21518) is entirely on
the even/size-bounded side. *Scoring:* (a) YES — exact branch-and-bound
over odd prime sets with reciprocal-sum windows, a determined last prime,
and a divisor-closure at the second-to-last level: precisely a
4-core exact-arithmetic job, with the 1996 record set on Maple;
(b) low scoop risk (30 years untouched, empty code repo, the active
people are on a different sub-problem); (c) extends BBBG 1996, feeds the
Agoh–Giuga structure theory, updates OEIS A007850's comment, and the same
engine gives the first odd bound beyond 8 for primary pseudoperfect /
all-prime Znám solutions (Erdős problem #313 territory). Built-in
positive controls with published answers exist for both equation signs:
the 12 known Giuga numbers with ≤ 8 factors and the 8 known primary
pseudoperfect numbers with ≤ 8 factors must be reproduced exactly by the
same engine before any frontier run is believed.

## Selection

**Odd Giuga numbers.** Candidate 1's decidable case is priced at
CPU-months by the revealed behavior of three specialist groups, and its
soft case races two of them; Candidate 2's feasible corner cannot be
scoop-checked against a three-week-old paper in exactly that family and
would be a third repo session in one micro-industry. Candidate 3 wins on
every criterion: the bottleneck is certified CPU-hours of exact
arithmetic (the record is 1996 Maple), the frontier is demonstrably
stale on the odd side while demonstrably alive on the even side (so the
object still matters), and one engine yields two labelled frontier
results plus two published-answer control reproductions. Session branch:
`claude/kind-bohr-p4n37d` (harness-designated; the dated-branch
convention was overridden by the environment's branch assignment).

**The result attempted today.** Prove, by exhaustive certified
branch-and-bound with exact integer arithmetic, that every odd Giuga
number has more than M prime factors for some M ≥ 14, i.e. match and then
beat the recorded bound (any M ≥ 15 is a strict improvement of a
30-year-old bound on a classical object); from the same engine, prove
that every odd primary pseudoperfect number (equivalently, every
all-prime solution of the improper Znám equation in odd primes) has more
than M′ prime factors for some M′ ≥ 9, the first explicit odd bound
beyond the trivial consequence of Butske–Jaje–Mayernik's r ≤ 8 census.
Achieved means: both even-side control censuses reproduced exactly,
frontier runs completed with committed certificates and a clean-room
second engine agreeing on an overlapping range, and the lemma layer
(equivalence, squarefreeness, integrality, the k ≥ 2 disposal, the
forced-3 lemma) written up with proofs in NOTE.md.
