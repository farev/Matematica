Computational research note · Gilbreath's conjecture

The fine structure of the Chase–Hunter–Tao constants: exact values c₄–c₆ and a two-parameter submask law

July 28, 2026 · produced in a Claude Code research session · code & certificates in the companion repository

Abstract. For the continuous model of Gilbreath's conjecture introduced by Chase, Hunter and Tao (arXiv 2607.08712, July 2026) — iterated absolute differences of i.i.d. Exp(1) data — we compute the expected depth-i entry ci exactly for i = 4, 5, 6, extending CHT's exact values, each with a computer-verified exactness certificate in ℚ. We prove the model's sign arrangement attains the maximal 2i(i+1)/2 chambers. A Monte Carlo to depth 4095, anchored on the exact values, shows that ci is governed by the binary expansion of i through a two-parameter submask law that explains the Lucas-theorem oscillation CHT observed, fits with R² ≈ 0.98 where a pure power law fails entirely, and renders their tentative conjecture ci ≍ 1/i untenable in the accessible range: partial sums grow like n1/5, not log n. On the rigorous side we prove a two-channel sharpening of CHT's key inequality: cn ≥ 2 exp(−Σk<n ck) for n ≥ 2, whence Σi≤n ci ≥ log(2n − 2 + e²).

1Setting

CHT define the Gilbreath array by a(0,j) = aj (i.i.d. Exp(1)) and a(i+1,j) = |a(i,j) − a(i,j+1)|; by stationarity ci := 𝐄 a(i,j) depends only on i. They compute c₀ = c₁ = 1, c₂ = 7/9, c₃ = 227/288, prove Σi≤n ci ≥ log(n+e) and the key inequality cn ≥ exp(−Σi<n ci), state that they cannot prove boundedness of (ci), and tentatively conjecture ci ≍ 1/i from a 10⁶-sample simulation to depth 20 (their Remark 1.5).

2Exact values, with certificates

Fixing the sign of every intermediate difference splits ℝi+1≥0 into polyhedral cones on which the depth-i entry is linear; exponential integrals over simplicial cones have closed rational forms. Rays come from exact integer double description; triangulations use floating Delaunay for combinatorics only, cross-checked by a second independent projection and repaired, on disagreement, by an exact pulling triangulation via integer rank tests. Certificate: the exponential measures of all chambers must sum to exactly 1 in ℚ — this identity holds exactly in every reported run, and the pipeline reproduces CHT's c₂ and c₃.

Proposition 1 (all sign histories occur). Every one of the 2i(i+1)/2 sign patterns of the intermediate differences is realized on a full-dimensional cone of initial data. Proof. Build the array bottom-up: set the bottom entry to 1; given row t+1 > 0 and the desired signs, put At1 = Tt and Atj+1 = Atj − st+1jAt+1j; taking Tt large makes row t positive and all constraints strict. ∎  The continuous model therefore has no combinatorial obstruction: cooling is irreducibly statistical. 3The submask law

A tiered Monte Carlo (40M samples to depth 63, decreasing to 150k at 4095; all exact anchors reproduced within 1.2σ) shows that log(i·ci) regressed on the individual binary digits of i yields R² = 0.95 with a cascade of per-bit factors:

The one-parameter match in the second row is the statement that a binary submask m of i contributes weight qm, via the identity Σm⊆i qm = Πj∈i(1 + q2^j). Fitting directly:

(⋆) ci ≈ C · i−α · Σm ⊆ i qm
q ≈ 0.68, α ≈ 0.80, C ≈ 1.14  ·  R² = 0.980 on i ∈ [64, 1023] (10-parameter free-bit model: 0.953; pure power law: R² < 0)
  • Adjacent indices differ by up to ×4.7 (c₅₁₁ vs c₅₁₂); at i = 1023 (ten ones), i·ci = 20.7, on the extrapolated digit-sum ladder.
  • Local decay exponent oscillates log-periodically: ≈ 0.93 along i = 2k, ≈ 0.63 along i = 2k−1.
  • Cross-validation: fitted on [64, 511], the law predicts [512, 1023] with R² = 0.93.
  • Out of sample: fitted on [64, 1023] only, the law predicts all 3072 new values in [1024, 4095] with R² = 0.90 (median |rel. error| 13%). The digit-sum ladder stays geometric at the new scale (11 rungs, ≈ 1.20 per bit; mean i·ci from 4.4 at s = 2 to 24.8 at s = 11) and adjacent indices now differ by ×6.8 (c₂₀₄₇ vs c₂₀₄₈). The one systematic deviation is informative: constant q under-predicts the extremes (all-ones measured 1.34–1.42× above, powers of two 0.73–0.83× below) — the oscillation amplitude keeps growing with scale; the truth is more digit-structured than (⋆), not less.

4Consequences for CHT's Remark 1.5

Partial sums fit Sn = 10.84·n1/5 − 11.43 to three decimals at every dyadic checkpoint n = 63 … 1023 (where log(n+e) ≤ 6.94, the CHT lower bound). Fitted on n ≤ 1023, the same formula then predicted S₂₀₄₇ = 38.37 and S₄₀₉₅ = 45.78; the deep run measured 38.33 and 45.60 — 0.1–0.4% accuracy two octaves out of sample. Under ci ≍ 1/i one would have Sn ~ log n; the observed growth is a clean power, consistent with the typical exponent 1 − α ≈ 0.2. The conjecture of Remark 1.5 is untenable in the computationally accessible range — though CHT's theorems only force Sn ≳ log n, so a turnover beyond this range remains logically possible.

5Mechanism: Lucas channels, activated by the tail

A large initial value at offset m propagates through |diff| dynamics as an exact Pascal-mod-2 pattern (the strip above) and reaches the apex iff C(i, m) is odd — iff m ⊆ i bitwise (Lucas). The submask sum in (⋆) is a sum over these open channels, with qm the measured attenuation of the channel at lateral offset m. Two confirmations:

  1. Starve the channels and the law dies. With Uniform[0,1] initial data (bounded tail) the oscillation nearly vanishes — c₁₂₇ ≈ c₁₂₈ within 1.5%, against ×3.1 apart for exponential data — and decay is a clean power law, α ≈ 0.92. Since the Cramér model of prime gaps has an exponential-type tail, the exponential model — where the law is strong — is the Gilbreath-relevant one.
  2. CHT's key inequality is the m = 0 channel — and the two outer channels can be used at once. Their Prop. 2.1 lower bound runs on a₁ − Σk a(k,2): exactly the leftmost channel.
Theorem 2 (two-channel lower bound). For every n ≥ 2,  cn ≥ 𝐄 e−(SL−+B) + 𝐄 e−(SR−+A) ≥ 2 exp(−Σk<n ck), where A = a(n−1,1), B = a(n−1,2), SL− = Σk≤n−2 a(k,2), SR− = Σk≤n−2 a(k,n−k). Consequently Σi≤n ci ≥ log(2n − 2 + e²), an asymptotic additive log 2 over CHT's Theorem 1.4.
Proof. The apex is exactly |A − B| = (A−B)⁺ + (B−A)⁺. CHT's telescoping at depth n−1 gives A ≥ a₁ − SL−; its mirror (the triangle is reversal-invariant) gives B ≥ an+1 − SR−. Now W = SL− + B involves only a₂,…,an+1, so it is independent of a₁, and 𝐄[(a₁ − W)⁺ | W] = e−W; Jensen with 𝐄SL− + 𝐄B = Σk<n ck gives the first term, and the mirrored argument the second. ∎  Elementary — CHT's own techniques plus reversal symmetry — but a strict sharpening of their Prop. 2.1, and the first bound using two Lucas channels simultaneously. Every step was additionally verified numerically at n = 6 on 4×10⁶ samples. The natural conjecture suggested by (⋆): all open channels contribute, cn ≳ (Σm⊆n qm)·exp(−Σk<n ck).

6Open questions

  1. Prove any strict digit-dependence of ci (e.g. c2^k+1 > c2^k for large k) — nothing of this kind is currently known.
  2. Extend Theorem 2 to middle channels or beat its constant — e.g. does cn ≥ (2+κ)e−Σc hold for odd n (channel m = 1 open)?
  3. Does (⋆) persist as i → ∞, or is it a transient? What are the true asymptotics of q and α?
  4. Exact c₇ (2²⁸ chambers) — needs symmetry reduction or a smarter integration scheme.
  5. The discrete Cramér-model analogue, and the route back to the primes.

7Related session results on Gilbreath's conjecture proper

The same session verified Gilbreath's conjecture rigorously for the first 455,052,510 rows (primes < 10¹⁰, via the propagation-lemma certificate), audited the two dangerous structures of CHT's deterministic Theorem 1.6 on the real primes — worst zero-block 31 vs threshold ~3×10¹² and worst {0,d}-block ratio 11 vs ~10²⁶, both growing at coin-flip rates — and measured the prime array's defect dynamics (×0.936/row decay under an exactly critical mean-field, with period-4 Sierpiński modulation).
