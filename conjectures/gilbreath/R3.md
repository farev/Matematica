# R3: the corridor program executed, and what killed it

*Session of 2026-07-29 (part 5). Companion to REDUCTION.md §3 (statement of
R2 and R3) and R3-ATTACK.md (the attack brief this session executed). CHT =
Chase, Hunter, Tao, arXiv 2607.08712, read in the original PDF during this
session; the reference resolves as cited, which also discharges the
repository's standing caveat about it.*

**Question (R3, REDUCTION.md §3).** Is eventual Gilbreath (leads in {0,1}
for all large n) a formal consequence of [fixed-order gap-pattern
asymptotics of the Cramér model] + [a_n = o(n)]?

**Outcome in one paragraph.** The brief's corridor idea (parity-steer the
erosion path so a small plant survives to the lead) is dead, but not for
the reason the brief feared. The corridor linear algebra is trivial (the
system is a triangular involution, always solvable, Lemma R3.2), and the
statistics are beatable to any fixed order (de Bruijn periods). What kills
it is dynamics, in two measured laws: a cooling race (a period-P parity
structure survives as a {0,2} phase only if P is below the local cooling
time; measured boundary P* ≍ μ^b, b = 0.84 ± 0.04 in the entry scale μ,
and P* matches the directly measured cooling time within 20%) and front
pinning (a {0,2} phase cannot invade a cooled {0,1} region; measured
penetration at most ~5 columns at every entry scale up to μ = 4096, with
interface value scale exactly 2 independent of μ; the residual transport
channel, single-value ballistic transit, has finite range ≈ 2V and is
killed at scale by a_n = o(n) via the now partly proved erosion bound,
Lemma R3.8). Together these seal every corridor-type route to a negative
answer for R3. On the other hand the same machinery, run at bounded period,
produces a strong insufficiency theorem: for every fixed K there is an
independent sequence with bounded entries and model-quality window
statistics up to order K whose leads exceed 1 at positive density forever.
This proves that axiom (ii) of CHT Theorem 1.3 (no concentration in
2-separated sets) is necessary, and necessary in a strong form: its failure
can be made invisible to every fixed-order window statistic. R3 itself
remains open but is reframed with the evidence now pointing to an
affirmative answer, and a three-step proof skeleton is identified.

## 1. The parity-transform machinery

Setting as in REDUCTION.md: top row a_0, a_1, ... nonnegative integers
(normalized gaps), iteration a_{(s+1,n)} = |a_{(s,n)} − a_{(s,n+1)}|, lead
of depth s is a_{(s,0)}. Background model: independent geometric(1−θ)−1,
θ = 1 − 2/log 10⁸ unless stated (mean μ ≈ 8.2). Parities: y_i = a_i mod 2.
CHT Lemma 3.10 (parity formula, classical Rule-90/Lucas): a_{(s,n)} ≡
Σ_t C(s,t) a_{n+t} mod 2. CHT remark after their Lemma 3.11 that they do
not use the parity formula directly; everything in this section is built
on it.

Let S be the Pascal-mod-2 (Sierpinski) matrix S[s,u] = C(s,u) mod 2 =
[u is a binary submask of s].

**Lemma R3.1 (transform identities). PROVED.** Over F₂:

(a) *Involution:* S² = I. (Σ_u C(s,u)C(u,r) = C(s,r)·2^{s−r}, which is even
unless s = r.)

(b) *Path identity:* for a plant at index m, the erosion-path cell
(s, m−1−s) has parity z_s, where z = S·rev(y_0..y_{m−1}). The corridor
constraint system of the brief IS the Sierpinski matrix.

(c) *Lead identity:* the lead parity at depth s is (S y)_s, with y the
prefix parities, left-anchored. The lead-parity sequence of a Gilbreath
array is the Sierpinski transform of its top parities.

(d) *Conjugation:* S σ S = I + σ, where σ is the shift. Differencing in
string space is shifting in transform space; consequently
S T_Δ S = (I+σ)^Δ, and a scale jump by Δ multiplies transform weight by at
most 2^{popcount(Δ)}.

(e) *Doubling:* for m a power of two, z^{(2m)} = (ζ, ζ + z^{(m)}) with
ζ = S·rev(new segment).

*Proofs:* (a) and the Pascal recurrence give the rest by direct
computation; all five were verified exactly at m up to 1024
(`r3_identities.py`, all PASS). ∎

**Lemma R3.2 (corridor classification). PROVED.** The brief's corridor
system (zero the path parities at depths s ≥ s_min) is always solvable:
S is lower triangular with unit diagonal, so the constrained system has
rank exactly m − s_min and solution dimension exactly s_min. For
s_min = 2^B the solution set is precisely the right-anchored 2^B-periodic
parity strings. In particular the brief's experiment-1 question ("if no
solution exists ... the corridor idea dies immediately") is settled in the
affirmative direction with no computation: solutions always exist, and
GF(2) elimination is unnecessary. ∎

**Corollary R3.3 (nilpotency). PROVED.** If y is 2^B-periodic on a window
(any phase), then all parity rows at depth ≥ 2^B vanish identically on the
window's dependency cone: (σ+1)^{2^B} = σ^{2^B} + 1 = 0 on 2^B-periodic
strings. The corridor does not merely zero the path; it zeroes every deep
parity in its cone, forcing all deep values there to be even. Content-free:
the period block's content is irrelevant. (Verified exactly, B = 3, 5.) ∎

## 2. Execution of the brief's experiment 1: the corridor self-destructs

`r3_corridor.py`, m = 1024, P = 32 de Bruijn block, conditioned geometric
values (a_i = 2·geometric(1−θ²) + y_i, exact conditional distribution),
20 seeds.

By Corollary R3.3 a genuinely cooled ({0,1}-valued) corridor cone would be
identically zero, hence the rows above it eventually constant: impossible
for randomly sampled magnitudes. The array must instead cool one 2-adic
level up, to {0,2}. Measured: deep path values have support {0,2(,4)},
P(path value = 2) = 0.32 ± 0.13, total erosion 730 ± 279 over 1024 rows
(0.71 per row), versus 0.56 per row for the i.i.d. control. Parity steering
does not reduce erosion at all. The brief's plant (V = 3× shallow erosion
= 306) arrives at the lead with value 1: dead. A plant of 3m = 3072 arrives
with 2547 and derails exactly at depth m = 1024, confirming the transport
mechanics.

The instructive failure: **the corridor needs no plant.** The periodic
prefix alone produces bad leads (value 2) at density 0.33 ± 0.11 (P = 32),
0.50 ± 0.01 (P = 2, the alternating string), at all depths in the periodic
cone. The plant was solving the wrong problem: the way to derail leads with
small entries is not to protect a large value, it is to make the lead
region itself two-valued.

## 3. The cooling race: when does a period-P structure survive?

`r3_nested.py` and `r3_boundary.py`. The {0,2} phase requires the parity
rows to die (depth P, Corollary R3.3) before the value field cools to
{0,1} (depth T_cool, growing with the entry scale μ). Otherwise the array
enters the all-even regime already ≤ 1, and even-and-≤1 means zero: the
lead is sealed by max-monotonicity (row maxima never increase).

Measured bad-lead densities at depths [4P, 32P], 3 seeds each
(`r3_boundary.py`; extended grid in `r3_boundary2.log`):

| P=512 | **0.000** | 0.03 | 0.37 | | | |

Refined fit (`r3_boundary_fit.py`, span 16, interpolated 0.05-density
crossings; μ = 256 de-censored by the P = 2048, 4096 runs in
`r3_boundary_decensor.log`): P*(μ) = 82, 165, 343, 476, 848, 1689 at
μ = 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, a factor 32 in μ. Free fit: **P* ≍ μ^b with
b = 0.839 ± 0.040** (prefactor 15.7, RSS 0.053). Every candidate closed
form is clearly rejected: μ^{2/3} (RSS 0.302), μ (RSS 0.272), μ/ln μ
(RSS 0.205); the earlier three-point law 24·μ^{2/3} does not survive. At
μ = 512 the transition smears (densities 0.13 and 0.09 ± 0.15 at P = 2048
and 4096), consistent with the heavy-tailed cooling times at that scale;
that point is excluded from the fit. Survival therefore costs entry scale
μ ≳ P^{1/b} ≈ P^{1.19}, polynomial either way. This is
far cheaper than the naive prediction: generic defect decay (×0.936/row,
`experiments.py`) would give a logarithmic boundary and predict death at
(P = 256, μ = 64), which is measured alive.

The mechanism is confirmed directly: measuring the pre-period band's own
cooling time T_hot(μ) (hot fraction < 10⁻³; huge-period runs so only the
band is seen) gives T_hot = 100, 435, 2072 at μ = 8, 64, 512, and
P*/T_hot = 0.82 and 1.09 at the two comparable points: the boundary is
the cooling race, P* ≈ T_hot. The slow cooling of the periodic band
relative to generic backgrounds is consistent with the fractal thinning of
Rule-90 odd-cell density on periodic data (single-defect rows have
2^{popcount(s)} odd cells), but the exponent b itself remains underived.
T_hot at μ = 512 is heavy-tailed across seeds (1220, 1328, 3668, with a
stalled hot pocket in the tail seed). Two further observations: the
density tends to 1 as μ → ∞ at fixed P (deep 2-adic towers: with huge
entries the lead is almost never ≤ 1), and persistence is stationary:
at (P, μ) = (8, 8) all twelve 4096-depth windows to depth 49,150 sit at
0.48 to 0.52 (both seeds), with only a warm-up transient at (32, 8).
Label: NUMERICAL.

The two-scale nested construction (stale head inside a fresh P₂ = 4096
block, `r3_nested.py`) is the clean kill: despite exact 4096-periodicity
(nilpotency applies in full), bad leads stop at depth 66 in every seed.
The pre-period band (depths < 4096) radiates generic parities, the array
cools to {0,1} everywhere, and the all-even regime arrives empty.

## 4. Front pinning: heat cannot be imported

`r3_front.py`. Top parities i.i.d. on columns [0, W) (the strip), period-32
de Bruijn on [W, N) (the ocean, a {0,2} phase). Strip widths W = 128 to
1024, N = 16W, 8 seeds each: the leftmost value-≥2 cell stays within ±2
columns of W for all N−1 rows, and not one bad lead occurs beyond the
shallow transient (one stray at depth 71 for W = 128, inside the
transient's tail). A {0,2} ocean cannot invade a cooled {0,1} strip:
value-2 cells can only be created from parents {2,0} or {3,1}, so hot
transport needs an even-parity ancestry chain with two-valued support, and
that percolation is measured deeply subcritical (penetration ≤ 2 columns
over 12,000+ rows). Label: NUMERICAL LAW; proving it is well-posed and
central (see §7).

**Stress test at large entry scale** (`r3_front2.py`, μ = 64, 512, 4096,
W = 256 and 1024, N = 16W). Pinning of the phase is robust: beyond the
transient, the minimum of ell(s) − W is −5 (W = 256) and −1 (W = 1024)
at every μ including 4096, the persistent interface value scale is
exactly 2 (median across seeds and checkpoints, independent of μ), and
erosion is mutual (the sea never eats the ocean either: the interface
proxy h(s) − W stays at median 0). But the test exposed a second
transport channel invisible at μ ≈ 8: **ballistic plant transit**. Rare
geometric-tail values of size a few times μ ride the left edge of their
light cone one column per row, eroding at a measured toll of 0.52 per row
through the cooled strip (a live traveler decayed 6530 → 6397 over 256
columns), deliver bad leads at depths up to ≈ 2.3μ regardless of strip
width, and then the array reseals permanently (all seeds quiet from depth
9611 to 15360 at μ = 4096, W = 1024). The two channels are physically
distinct: phase-boundary motion (pinned, range 0) versus single-value
ballistics (finite range ≈ 2V in the value V, set by the telescope toll,
not by any interface property). Under a_n = o(n) the ballistic channel
dies at scale: that is exactly Theorem R2's sharpness calculus, and its
strip half is now provable (Lemma R3.8 below). Label: NUMERICAL.

Consequence, combined with §3: bad leads at depth s require parity
degeneracy of bounded period touching the lead's own O(1)-neighborhood at
scale s, or a ballistic traveler of value Ω(s), excluded under o(n)
entries. Structure sitting to the right of a generic band, however
massive, never reaches column 0. This is what killed the layered
construction
(`r3_hierarchical.py`): its exact parity replication law (lead parity at
depth s in segment l equals lead parity at depth s mod 2^{c_{l−1}};
verified exactly, T1 PASS) faithfully replays even-parity patches at all
scales, and the value field under them is 0 instead of 2: the level-2
material died in the generic bands between segments. Bad leads stop at
depth ~1024 = the first segment's exhaustion, in all 10 seeds.

## 5. What the machinery proves: insufficiency at every finite order

**Proposition R3.4 (bounded-entry failure of Gilbreath, no statistics).
PROVED (using CHT Thm 1.2).** Let a_n = 2b_n with b_n i.i.d.
geometric(1−θ)−1. Entries are bounded in probability, a_n = o(n) a.s.,
and a.s. the leads exceed 1 infinitely often. *Proof.* The array is 2×(the
Gilbreath array of b), and CHT Theorem 1.2/1.3 gives b's leads eventually
in {0,1}. b's lead parity at depth 2^j is b̂_0 ⊕ b̂_{2^j} (parity formula:
C(2^j, t) is odd iff t ∈ {0, 2^j}), so across j these are independent
nondegenerate bits given b̂_0: by Borel–Cantelli II, b's leads are odd,
hence equal to 1, infinitely often; the original leads equal 2 there. ∎
This makes precise CHT's remark after Theorem 1.3 that with all-even
entries "the conclusion could then very well be false". It is elementary,
but we could not find it stated anywhere.

**Theorem R3.5 (insufficiency of every fixed order, bounded entries).
Construction and statistics PROVED; bad-lead persistence NUMERICAL.**
For every K there is a sequence of independent random variables a_n with:

(α_K) for every k ≤ K and every pattern v, the window counts up to x equal
x·p_v + O_k(√(x log log x)): the same error quality the i.i.d. Cramér model
itself enjoys (LIL scale). Construction: parities forced to a de Bruijn
cycle of order K (period P = 2^K), values exactly conditioned geometric
with entry scale μ ≳ P^{1.2} ≈ 2^{1.2K} (the measured boundary of §3, so
singly exponential in K). The parity-signature counts of every k ≤ K
window are exactly uniform per period (de Bruijn), so value-pattern counts
are sums of independent indicators with the correct means: deviation
O_k(P) + LIL fluctuation.

(β) entries are O_P(1) (bounded scale for each fixed K; grossly o(n) and
Cramér-compatible);

(γ) leads exceed 1 at positive density of depths (measured 0.29 to 0.97
across the viable grid of §3, stationary out to depth 49,150 with no decay
across twelve consecutive windows; all deep cells are even by Corollary
R3.3, which is PROVED, and the halved array's leads hit 1 at positive
density, which is the NUMERICAL step).

Consequently no axiom system of the form [order-≤K window asymptotics with
model-quality errors] + [any entry bound down to O(1)] implies eventual
Gilbreath, for any fixed K. Compare Theorem R2 (REDUCTION.md): R2 achieved
all orders simultaneously but needed single entries of size n² (grossly
non-Cramér); R3.5 stays bounded and loses only the orders above K. The
trade surface is now mapped: (order reach K, entry size) with (∞, n²)
[R2] and (K, exp(c·2^K)) [R3.5] both attainable, and R3 asking for
(∞, o(n)).

**Positioning against CHT.** Their Theorem 1.3 proves eventual Gilbreath
for independent sequences under (i) a_n ≤ δn and (ii) no concentration in
2-separated sets. R3.5's sequences are independent, satisfy (i) with any
margin, violate (ii) at every position (each a_n is parity-pinned), and
their leads fail infinitely often: so (ii) is necessary in Theorem 1.3.
The strong form is the point: the violation of (ii) is invisible to every
window statistic of order ≤ K. Membership in a 2-separated set is a
pointwise, unbounded-order property; no finite-order marginal can see it.
This is the sharpest expression yet of the R2 lesson ("Gilbreath needs
unbounded-length input").

## 6. The noose: why the negative route dies

Every route to a negative R3 answer through parity degeneracy now faces a
three-mechanism conjunction:

1. **Bounded-period statistics (PROVED).** A parity structure of period P
   at the lead violates fixed-order statistics at k = log₂P + 1 with
   deviations linear in x (missing signatures; measured: single-period
   P = 32 control has k = 6 deviation growing 258 → ~1000 from x = 4k to
   16k while the k ≤ 5 deviations stay CLT-sized). So the period visible at
   the lead must grow without bound along the sequence.

2. **The cooling race (NUMERICAL, §3).** A period-P structure at the lead
   survives as a {0,2} phase only if P ≲ T_cool(local entry scale). The
   lead's entries are a fixed prefix of the sequence, so T_cool at the lead
   is a fixed constant: growing periods self-cool and arrive empty. (Exact
   corridors also collapse under stacking: two exact periodicities of the
   same prefix collapse to the smaller period by Fine and Wilf, and the
   doubling identity R3.1(e) shows the only weight-preserving dyadic
   extension is exact repetition. PROVED.)

3. **Front pinning (NUMERICAL, §4; ballistic half proved conditionally).**
   Structure placed away from the lead, with the statistics repaired in
   between, cannot ship its heat leftward across the repaired band: the
   phase boundary is pinned at all tested entry scales, and the only other
   channel, single-value ballistic transit, has range ≈ 2V (Lemma R3.8),
   which o(n) entries cannot sustain at scale.

A corridor-type counterexample must have unbounded lead periods (by 1),
which self-cool (by 2), and cannot be fed from the right (by 3). The
brief's stated hope for the negative route is therefore dead unless a
construction evades all three simultaneously, and we see no remaining
geometry: the plant variant died in §2, the layered variant in §4, the
nested variant in §3.

What survives of the brief's "over-determination obstruction" intuition:
its conclusion (corridors cannot beat the statistics at all orders) was
right, but the mechanism is dynamical, not rank-based (the systems are
always solvable, Lemma R3.2) and not primarily statistical (any fixed
order is beatable, Theorem R3.5).

## 7. R3 reframed, and a proof skeleton for the affirmative

R2's axiom (α) as written is a coupling statement (counts within
O_k(log log x) of an i.i.d. realization). Hardy–Littlewood-type conjectures
are asymptotics, not couplings; the model's own realization quality is
LIL, √(x log log x). The two readings genuinely differ and should be kept
apart:

- **R3-strong** (coupling budget O_k(log log x)): every parity-pinning
  construction is banned outright (pinning Θ(x) parities forces count
  drifts far above log log x). All of this session's counterexample
  machinery is unavailable. Open, and plausibly provable affirmative
  before R3-weak is resolved.

- **R3-weak** (asymptotics budget O_k(√(x log log x)), all fixed k, plus
  a_n = o(n)): this is where the session's war was fought. Every finite
  order fails (Theorem R3.5); all-orders-simultaneously stands, guarded by
  the noose of §6. Evidence now points affirmative, reversing the brief's
  corridor hope.

**Lemma R3.6 (lead-parity rigidity). PROVED.** Let y be the top parities
and Λ = S y the lead-parity sequence (Lemma R3.1(c)). If Λ_s = 0 for all
s ≥ s₀ (s₀ ≥ 1), then y is 2^B-periodic on all of ℕ, B = ⌈log₂ s₀⌉,
meaning y_{u+2^B} = y_u for every u ≥ 0 (the minimal period may be
smaller). In fact the clean biconditional holds: y is 2^B-periodic iff
Λ_s = 0 for all s ≥ 2^B, and the threshold 2^B is exact.
*Proof.* S is an involution (row-finite, so no convergence issue), so
y = S Λ with Λ supported on [0, s₀): y_u = Σ_{r < s₀, r ⊆ u} Λ_r. Each
indicator [r ⊆ u] with r < 2^B depends only on the low B bits of u, hence
y_u is a function of u mod 2^B. The converse is Corollary R3.3 applied at
the lead. ∎ (Verified exactly in both directions at s₀ = 5, 16, 100 in
`r3_identities.py`, and adversarially audited on 4000 random cases
including all edge cases, with tightness: in most trials y is not
2^{B−1}-periodic, and Λ_{2^B−1} ≠ 0 occurs at frequency ~0.44 for random
periodic y, so neither threshold can be improved.)

**Corollary R3.7 (odd leads are forced). PROVED.** Any gap sequence
satisfying weak-(α) at every fixed order has leads of odd value at
infinitely many depths. *Proof.* If not, Λ_s = 0 for all s ≥ some s₀, so
by R3.6 the parities are 2^B-periodic; a 2^B-periodic parity string has at
most 2^B distinct order-(B+1) parity signatures out of 2^{B+1}, so some
signature's expected count x·2^{−(B+1)} is missed entirely: a deviation
linear in x at the fixed order B+1, contradicting weak-(α). ∎

This is the first unconditional structural consequence extracted from the
full statistics hierarchy at the lead: it eliminates the "leads eventually
even" failure mode outright, and every counterexample constructed in this
session (all-even, alternating, de Bruijn periodic) is of exactly that
mode, consistent with each failing weak-(α) at one order. What the
corollary does not do is bound the odd values by 1, or exclude bad leads
interleaved with odd ones: that interleaved regime is where the dynamical
mechanisms (cooling race, front pinning) take over.

**Proof skeleton for R3-weak affirmative** (the sharpest formulation this
program has produced, replacing R3-ATTACK.md):

(S1) *Front pinning as a theorem*, now split into the two channels that
`r3_front2.py` separated:

(S1a) *Phase confinement.* In a Gilbreath array whose top parities are
generic on a strip [0, W) and arbitrary beyond, the {0,2}-phase boundary
does not advance: value-≥2 cells whose hot ancestry carries only O(1)
values meet columns [0, W − C] only at depths O(T_cool). Two pieces of
this are now PROVED by re-aiming CHT's own machinery (derivation by this
session's theory agent; both reduce to their results):

**Theorem R3.9 (interior strip cooling; corollary of CHT Prop. 4.1).**
Let the strip entries a_0..a_{W−1} be independent, {0,...,D₀}-valued,
each with sup over 2-separated A of P(a_i ∈ A) ≤ 1 − ε, and let the
entries beyond be arbitrary. With probability ≥ 1 − W^{−A}, every cell
(s, n) with s ≥ C ε^{−1}(D₀ log(eW/D₀) + A log W) and n + s < W has
value ≤ 1. *Proof:* such a cell is the apex of a triangle whose light
cone lies inside the strip; CHT Proposition 4.1 bounds each apex, and a
union over the ≤ W²/2 cells finishes. Consequence (hot-parent lemma):
every hot cell below that depth roots its hot ancestry in the ocean or
in the last O(ε^{−1} D₀ log W) columns of the strip; heat cannot root in
the bulk of a generic strip.

**Theorem R3.10 (fast-crossing suppression).** In the ≤ 2-valued regime
(hot transport by value-2 cells with off-path parents ≤ 2), each
leftward step of a transport path forces its off-path parent to value 0,
hence parity 0; these parity forms have pairwise distinct leading
columns (coefficient C(s,0) = 1), so conditionally on the entire ocean
they are jointly uniform bits, and a path crossing L columns pays 2^{−L}
against path entropy 2^{T·H(L/T)}. Crossings at speed v > v* are
exponentially suppressed in L, where v* ≈ 0.7729 is the root of
H(v) = v. The ballistic channel (speed 1) is in particular excluded
within the bounded-value phase.

**The remaining gap, named (Open Lemma R3.11, persistent-alignment
cost).** Slow crossings (v < v*) beat the union bound because the parity
forms at column c depend on depth only through s mod 2^{⌈log₂(W−c)⌉}
(Lucas), so the adversarial ocean eventually faces no fresh constraints
at a given column and can schedule its boundary to the favorable
residues. The missing renewal statement: occupying column W−j on an
unbounded set of usable rows costs j affine parity equations determined
by the ocean alone, whence P(penetration ever ≥ j) ≤ C 2^{−cj} uniformly
in time. The same finite-budget count already predicts the measured Θ(1)
penetration (observed ≤ 2 to 5 columns at all μ). This lemma is now the
single analytic core separating the noose from a theorem.

(S1b) *Ballistic erosion bound.* PROVED conditionally:

**Lemma R3.8.** Let a traveler of value V ride the left edge of its light
cone from position p across a region where (i) the array values on its
path cells are in {0,1} (cooled), and (ii) the top parities on the columns
those path cells depend on are i.i.d. uniform. Then the erosion over d
consecutive path cells satisfies P(erosion ≤ d/2 − t) ≤ d/(4t²); in
particular a traveler with V ≤ d/2 − d^{2/3} fails to cross, except with
probability ≤ 4 d^{−1/3}. *Proof.* In a {0,1}-valued region, value =
parity, and the path-cell parities are z_s = Σ_{t ⊆ s} y_{p−1−s+t}
(Lemma R3.1(b)): F₂-linear functionals of the top parities. Each contains
the fresh leftmost bit y_{p−1−s} (t = 0 is a submask of every s), which
no shallower path functional contains, so all d functionals are distinct
and nonzero even after restriction to the strip columns; distinct nonzero
linear functionals of i.i.d. uniform bits are pairwise independent
Bernoulli(1/2). The erosion is their sum, with mean d/2 and variance d/4
by pairwise independence; Chebyshev finishes. ∎ (Measured toll on a live
traveler: 0.52 per row.) The unconditional gap is hypothesis (i): it is
supplied by (S1a), so (S1a) is the remaining core.

(S2) *Inverse theorem at the lead.* If leads exceed 1 at depth s, then
(given S1) the top parities on [0, O(T_cool · something)] carry a
2-separated/periodic structure of period P = O(1) at scale s: the CHT
inverse theorem (their Theorem 1.6 obstruction list: zero blocks and
{0,d} blocks) localized to the lead's neighborhood, plus Corollary R3.3
to convert survival into bounded period.

(S3) *Bounded period contradicts (α) at one fixed order.* PROVED today
(mechanism 1 of §6).
