# PAGE handoff — powerful-progressions (new page)

No page exists for this conjecture; this is a new-page handoff.

## 1. Headline

**CERTIFIED** — the complete census of three-term arithmetic progressions of
*consecutive* powerful numbers now reaches 10^19: exactly **346** triples,
which are just **16 genuinely different triples** up to scaling — five orders
of magnitude beyond the published table (18 triples below 10^14, van Doorn,
May 2026) that is the entire prior evidence for the conjecture that
infinitely many exist.

## 2. Contributions

1. **CERTIFIED.** Complete census to 10^19: 346 triples (152 below 10^18;
   18 below 10^14, reproducing van Doorn's table count exactly as a
   calibration gate). Each of the 346 verified by an independent
   implementation (346/346); 10^18 prefix replicated with two independent
   segmentations; powerful-number counts match OEIS A118896 at 10^10–10^12.
2. **PROVED (Lemma 1′).** An exact criterion for which integer multiples of
   a gcd-1 integer triple are componentwise powerful: primes appearing with
   exponent 1 must divide the multiplier, and every prime of the multiplier
   must appear with exponent ≥ 2 − (its minimum exponent in the triple).
3. **CERTIFIED (structure).** The 346 triples fall into 16 scaling classes,
   each generated by a gcd-1 root: (1728, 1764, 1800) = 36·(48, 49, 50), and
   36 is precisely that root's minimal admissible multiplier; one root is an
   AP with common difference 7 at height 2.8×10^7. One class contributes 272
   of the 346 triples.
4. **CERTIFIED (saturation fails).** Scaling a triple does not keep it
   consecutive forever: the dominant chain first loses multiplier m = 288;
   the smallest failure anywhere is 2·(1728, 1764, 1800) = (3456, 3528,
   3600), broken by the powerful intruder 3481 = 59². So the naive route to
   the conjecture (scale one triple forever) provably dies; what's needed is
   a density statement.
5. **PROVED (Lemma 2) + CERTIFIED.** Two perfect squares in a consecutive
   triple force gap > √x; and 15 of the 16 primitives contain exactly one
   perfect square. The sixteenth — P15 at 4.15×10^17 — contains **none**:
   the first known squareless triple, invisible below the old horizon.
6. **CERTIFIED.** No four-term AP of consecutive powerful numbers exists
   below 10^19.
7. **NUMERICAL.** Primitive count grows 6 → 16 from 10^14 to 10^19 — new
   primitives arrive across the whole range; supports, does not prove,
   infinitude.
